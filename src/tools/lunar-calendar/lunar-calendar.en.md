## How to convert between solar and lunar dates

1. Pick **Solar to Lunar** if you have a regular calendar date, or **Lunar to Solar** if you have a lunar date.
2. For **Solar to Lunar**, choose a date in **Solar date**.
3. For **Lunar to Solar**, enter **Lunar day**, **Lunar month** and **Lunar year**. Tick **Leap month** if the date belongs to a leap month.
4. Read the result: the converted date, the weekday, the year, month and day names in the Can Chi (sexagenary) cycle, such as the year Binh Ngo, and whether the lunar month is a leap month.

## Why people look up lunar dates

- **Death anniversaries (giỗ):** Vietnamese families remember ancestors on the lunar date of their passing. Use **Lunar to Solar** to see which day it falls on this year.
- **Lunar birthdays:** many older Vietnamese still celebrate birthdays by the lunar calendar.
- **Full moon and 1st day of the month:** use **Solar to Lunar** to see whether today is the 1st or the 15th (rằm) of the lunar month, when many families make offerings.
- **Weddings and other big events:** many families check the lunar date and its Can Chi name before choosing a day.

## How the Vietnamese lunar calendar works

The Vietnamese calendar is lunisolar: each month starts on the day of a new moon, and leap months keep the year in step with the solar terms. This converter follows Ho Ngoc Duc's astronomical algorithm and computes new moons and solar terms in Vietnamese time (UTC+7), giving accurate results from about 1900 to 2100.

> Note: because it uses UTC+7, results can occasionally differ from Chinese calendars by a day or a month, as happened with Tet 1985. For Vietnamese holidays and family occasions, use a calendar computed for Vietnamese time like this one.

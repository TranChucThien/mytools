## How to use the loan calculator

1. Enter the **Loan amount**, for example 10,000.
2. Enter the **Interest rate (% per year)**, for example 12 or 7.5.
3. Enter the **Term (months)**, for example 12, 60 or 360.
4. Choose a method:
   - **Declining balance**: equal principal each month, interest on the remaining balance.
   - **Fixed monthly payment**: the same payment every month, covering interest first and principal with the rest.
   - **Flat rate**: interest every month is calculated on the original amount.
5. Read the **First payment**, **Total interest** and **Total repayment**, and scroll through the **Repayment schedule** to see principal, interest, payment and remaining balance for every period.

## Loan formulas

| Method | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Declining balance | `Principal per month = P ÷ n` |
| | `Interest = Remaining balance × r ÷ 12` |
| Fixed monthly payment | `Payment = P × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i) ^ −n)`, where `i = r ÷ 12` |
| Flat rate | `Interest per month = P × r ÷ 12` (same every month) |

P is the loan amount, r the annual rate as a decimal and n the number of months.

## Flat rate vs declining balance: a worked example

Borrow 10,000 for 12 months at 12% per year:

| Method | First payment | Total interest | Total repayment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Declining balance | 933.33 | 650.00 | 10,650.00 |
| Fixed monthly payment | 888.49 | 661.85 | 10,661.85 |
| Flat rate | 933.33 | 1,200.00 | 11,200.00 |

The first payment is the same for declining balance and flat rate, which is why flat rate offers can look harmless. The difference shows up later. On a declining balance, interest falls from 100.00 in month 1 to 8.33 in month 12 as you pay the loan down. On a flat rate, you pay 100.00 interest every single month as if you still owed the full 10,000.

In this example the flat rate nearly doubles the interest. To get the same monthly payment with a fixed-payment loan, the rate would have to be about 21.5% per year. When a lender quotes "just 1% a month", ask whether interest is charged on the remaining balance or on the original amount.

> Tip: compare loans by total interest and total repayment, not only by the quoted rate or the first payment.

## How to use the savings interest calculator

1. Enter the **Deposit amount**, for example 10,000.
2. Enter the **Interest rate (% per year)**, for example 5 or 4.5.
3. Enter the **Term (months)**, for example 6 or 12.
4. Choose a method:
   - **Simple interest**: interest is paid on the original deposit only, typically all at maturity, as with many certificates of deposit and term deposits.
   - **Compounded monthly**: interest is added to the balance every month and earns interest itself.
5. Read the **Interest earned** and the **Total at maturity**.

## Savings interest formulas

| Method | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Simple interest | `Interest = P × r × months ÷ 12` |
| Compounded monthly | `Total = P × (1 + r ÷ 12) ^ months` |

Here P is the deposit and r is the annual rate as a decimal (5% = 0.05). For compounding, interest earned is the total minus the deposit.

## Examples

A 10,000 deposit at 5% per year:

| Term | Simple interest | Compounded monthly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 months | 250.00 | about 252.62 |
| 12 months | 500.00 | about 511.62 |

At 4.5% for 12 months, the same deposit earns 450.00 with simple interest and about 459.40 when compounded monthly, for a total of about 10,459.40.

> Tip: rates vary by bank, term and deposit size, and many term accounts charge a penalty or pay a lower rate if you withdraw early. Read the account terms before you lock your money in.

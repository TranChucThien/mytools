## How to flip a coin online

1. Agree first: who calls **Heads**, who calls **Tails**, or which choice each side stands for.
2. Press **Flip coin**. The result, **Heads** or **Tails**, appears instantly.
3. The tally below counts Heads and Tails for this session. Press **Reset** to start again from zero.

## How it works, and why it's fair

A real coin can be slightly biased by how it's held, how hard it's flipped or where it lands. This tool uses your browser's secure random number generator (crypto.getRandomValues), so each side has exactly a 50% chance on every flip.

The key idea: **every flip is independent**. Even after five Heads in a row, the sixth flip is still 50/50. The chance of 10 Heads in a row is 1 in 1,024 (about 0.1%), which sounds rare, but once you already have nine Heads, the tenth flip is still just 50%.

## Popular uses

- **Deciding who goes first**: kickoff, serve, or the first move in a board game.
- **Settling a choice**: pizza or tacos, this movie or that one.
- **Teaching probability**: have students flip 50 or 100 times, look at the tally and discuss why it is not a perfect split.
- **Picking sides** quickly for a group game.

## Tips

- Agree on what Heads and Tails mean **before** you flip to avoid arguments.
- Flip once and accept the result. "Best of three" only makes sense if everyone agreed to it up front.
- A classic trick: if you're torn, flip a coin. If you feel disappointed by the result, you probably already know what you want.

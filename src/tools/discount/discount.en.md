## How to use the discount calculator

1. Enter the **Original price**.
2. Enter the **Discount (%)**, for example 25 for 25% off.
3. If a coupon or member deal is taken off the sale price, enter it in **Extra discount (%)**. Otherwise leave it blank.
4. You instantly see the **Final price**, how much **You save** and the real **Total discount**.

## Discount formulas

| Value | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Price after one discount | `Original × (100 − Discount) ÷ 100` |
| Price after extra discount | `First sale price × (100 − Extra) ÷ 100` |
| You save | `Original − Final price` |
| Total discount | `You save ÷ Original × 100` |

**Why 30% + 20% is 44%, not 50%.** The second discount is applied to the reduced price. You pay 70% of the original, then 80% of that: 0.7 × 0.8 = 0.56, or 56% of the original. That makes the total discount 100% − 56% = **44%**. Only a single 50% discount takes half off the original price.

## Examples

- **Sale plus coupon:** a $350 coat at 30% off with an extra 20% coupon → 350 × 0.7 × 0.8 = **$196**. You save $154, a 44% total discount.
- **Single discount:** a $120 pair of shoes at 25% off → $90, you save $30.
- **Stacked member deal:** the same shoes with an extra 10% member discount → 90 × 0.9 = $81. You save $39, a 32.5% total discount.

> Shopping tip: when a store offers "an extra 20% off sale prices", the deal is smaller than it sounds. Run the numbers here before you decide it is the best price.
